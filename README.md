# Sprint-Review-Retrospective
Demonstration of Agile &amp; Scrum Practices used over the course of the SNHU Travel Project for the CS-250 (Software Development Lifecycle) course.

**How do I interpret user needs & implement them into a program? How does creating "user stories" help with this?**
  In Scrum-Agile, user needs are interpretted through the use of Epics & User Stories. Epics are large bodies of work that describe intended feature sets and functionality for a product. These, however, tend to be too large to develop in single iterative Agile Development cycles, also known as Sprints. As a result, for ease of development, they tend to be further broken down into simpler single feature descriptions from an end-user perspective, also know as User Stories. These simplified explanations for product features are then used to inform Developers on the intended end-product that users or stakeholders invision. This helps to provide direction for development in Agile-Scrums short incremental development cycles (aka Sprints) & helps to better focus their efforts when developing the intended feature.

**How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?**
  In my future development work, I plan to incorporate some of the base developmental philosophies of the Agile-Scrum methodology. Namely, the use of short iterative development cycles (aka Sprints), continual end-user/stakeholder feedback for product/feature refinement, and the use of Sprint Reviews & Retrospectives for development review & improvement. The encorporation of Sprint based incremental development is a fairly easy addition to my developmental repertoire from the gget go. With most of my developmental experience currently being in Software & Game Development, I've already adopted the habit of breaking down intended end products into smaller actionable items for development. For the inclusion of end-user/stakeholder feedback, I would likely begin by providing minimal viable versions of developing software for free with the intended hope of recieving public critiques of my work and where possible improvements could be made. And from this public criticism, I would develop User Stories which would then be used a development framework to create incremental updates to my software. And finally, in the implementation of Sprint Reviews & Retrospectives, I would include a review period at the end of each software update to take stock and review my complete work. Were the updates well recieved by the public? Where were mistakes or wasted development time made over the course of it's development? How can I best avoid these mistakes in future Sprint development cycles? And through this regular review & retrospective, I hope to make incremental improvements to my work flow, further optimizing my development pprocess & improving the overall quality of my future work.

**What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?**
  In the Scrum-Agile methodology, being a good team member means being open in your communication, collaborative in the development process, adaptable to developmental changes made by either Product Owners or end-users, & being able to promote an atmosphere of continual improvement & workflow refinement. As the name implies, Agile needs team members that a fluid & able to adapt to change. It also means being open to joint collaboration & transparent communication. In such a methodology, it is not enough to just turn in work on time from a siloed off corner of the development project. You've got to be open to criticism from coworkers & being willing to allow change & recommendations towards your completed work. Agile as a methodology abhors the concepts of complacency & stagnation. For this reason, any good team member in an Scrum-Agile based software development project is anyone that is adaptable, communicative, & willing to help in not only their own self improvement, but also the improvement of others. That what it means to be a good Scrum-Agile team member.
